HOW TO USE THIS FILE

An ADR (Architecture Decision Record) is just a short, dated note that says what you decided
and why — so future-you (or anyone else) doesn't have to reverse-engineer the reasoning from
the code six months later.

1. Copy this file to docs/decisions/NNN-short-title.md, where NNN is the next number
   (001, 002, 003...) and short-title is a few kebab-case words, e.g. 003-skip-mechanism.md
2. Fill in the four sections below. Keep each one to a paragraph — if it's growing into an
   essay, the decision is probably too big and wants splitting into two.
3. Set the Status line and leave it. Don't edit an old ADR's Decision or Consequences once
   it's marked Decided — if you change your mind later, write a NEW ADR that says so in its
   Context ("supersedes 003") and mark the old one's status "Superseded by 007". The history
   of changing your mind is useful; overwriting it isn't.
4. Delete this comment block before committing.
-->

# NNN — Short decision title

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Context
What needed deciding, why it came up now, and any constraint that shapes the answer
(your experience level, time available, what already exists in the code).

## Decision
What you chose, in one or two sentences. No hedging — this is the thing you're doing.

## Alternatives considered
The other options, each in a line or two: what it was, and the specific reason it lost out.
Not exhaustive — just enough that a future reader can tell you actually weighed something.

## Consequences
What this makes easier, what it makes harder, and what it rules out or commits you to.
This is the section most worth being honest in — it's the part that saves future-you from
re-litigating the same decision.
